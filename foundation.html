THE FOUNDATION

Mafakathini, South Africa

For my Eagle Scout project, I led the renovation of a school for disabled children in Mafakathini, South Africa. Together with the community, we raised over $10,000 to make lasting improvements for the students.

The teacher explained that for these children, this wasn't just a school. It was one of the only places where they could feel loved and cared for, because their opportunities in the outside world were so limited. That's what made this matter — it wasn't just about desks or murals. It was about creating a space that showed them they belonged.

What the project included:

  • Desks and chairs so kids no longer had to sit on the floor
  • Electricity installed and insulation added for cold months
  • A fully stocked kitchen with utensils and cooking equipment
  • Renovated bathrooms and a sidewalk for wheelchair access
  • Painted all classrooms and created bright murals
  • Personalized backpacks and school supplies for every child
  • Sleeping pads so kids could rest during the day
  • Playground swings, games, and toys
  • A community garden to provide fresh food

Eagle Scout — 2018. Featured in Scouting Magazine.

Project · Build Log

The Full Story

Funding came first. I spoke at a local church and organized a community spaghetti dinner to get the project off the ground. A local scouting organization donated 20 desks and chairs. A teacher packed 20 backpacks full of supplies. Conversations with community members and local officials helped spread the word. The project raised money in pieces — sometimes putting out calls for the last few hundred dollars while the team was already on the ground working.

The build ran eight days. Day one was cleaning — washing walls, pulling broken windows, fixing the water line, hauling in mulch. Each day added a layer. Electrical work. Stone pathways. Ceiling tiles and insulation. Alphabet murals painted by hand. A swing set assembled. Kitchen cupboards installed.
