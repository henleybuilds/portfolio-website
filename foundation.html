THE FOUNDATION

Mafakathini, South Africa

Before any of the automation work, there was a school in South Africa that needed help.

A school for children with disabilities in a village of 5,000.
No running water. No kitchen. One exposed lightbulb.
A spaghetti dinner fundraiser in Tennessee. Backpacks and school supplies collected. Desks donated by a local scouting organization. It got off the ground because people showed up.
Eight days. Walls painted, windows replaced, electrical work done, bathroom and kitchen installed, roof and floors repaired, pathways laid, insulation put in, murals painted, and a swing set built.
Eagle Scout — 2018. Featured in Scouting Magazine.

Project · Build Log

The Full Story

It started the way a lot of good things do — with a fundraiser. A $5 spaghetti dinner at a church hall in Tennessee. That paid for some of it. A local scouting organization donated desks and chairs. A school teacher packed backpacks full of supplies. A conversation with a public official at a banquet helped spread the word. The project raised money in pieces, sometimes putting out calls for the last few hundred dollars while the team was already on the ground working.

The build ran eight days. Day one was cleaning — washing walls, pulling broken windows, fixing the water line, bringing in mulch. Each day added a layer. Electrical work. Stone pathways laid. Ceiling tiles and insulation put in. Alphabet murals painted by hand. A swing set assembled. Kitchen cupboards installed. Curtains hung.

On the last day, the keys were handed back at a ribbon cutting.
