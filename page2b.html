Lego Instructions

Two presentations. Two different approaches to the same problem — how do you teach someone to build a Zap without writing them a wall of text.

The first one: I built a workflow that took any 5-step Zap and automatically generated a slide deck to walk through it step by step. The theme was Lego — each step styled like a building instruction, bricks and all.

The second one I wanted to go fully visual. No written instructions at all. A Lego minifigure at a computer with a bow and arrow pointing to exactly where to click — each slide a visual cue instead of a written step.

Jiu-Jitsu Training Assistant

After class I'd have a general sense of what I struggled with but nothing specific enough to actually act on. A week later it was gone.

I built a Slack bot that let me leave a quick voice note after training. It logged what I felt good about and what I got caught on. Before the next session it would send me a message — here's what you said was giving you trouble last time, focus on this today.

Simple loop. Voice note in, targeted reminder out.

Project · Build Log

Bricks for Paws

I wanted to build something that raised money for a local animal shelter.

I built a workflow that pulled all the adoptable dogs from the shelter database, logged their facts and personality traits, then posted one dog every day — photo, traits, a short write-up — with a call for people to donate.

The automation handled everything after the initial setup. The goal was simple: more visibility, more donations, more support for the shelter.
